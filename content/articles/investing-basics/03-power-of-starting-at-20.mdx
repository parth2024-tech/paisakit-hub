Most 21-year-olds think: *"I only earn ₹25,000 or ₹35,000 right now. What is the point of investing ₹2,000 a month? I'll wait until I am 30 and earning ₹1.5 Lakh per month, then invest ₹25,000 a month."*

This is the single most expensive mathematical misunderstanding in personal finance.

In <Term id="compounding">Compound Interest</Term>, **time is an exponent, while money is merely a multiplier**.

### The Tale of Two Friends: Rahul vs Priya

Let us compare two college graduates who follow different financial paths. Assume both achieve a reasonable 12% average annual return in equity index funds until age 60:

#### Rahul (Starts at Age 20)
- Starts an automated SIP of **₹5,000 every month** at age 20.
- Keeps investing ₹5,000/month consistently until age 60 (40 years).
- **Total money invested out of pocket:** ₹24,00,000 (₹24 Lakh).
- **Final wealth at age 60:** **₹5,94,00,000 (₹5.94 Crore)**.

#### Priya (Waits until Age 30)
- Decides to enjoy her 20s and delays investing until age 30.
- Realizing she is behind, she invests **₹15,000 every month**—three times what Rahul invested—for 30 years until age 60.
- **Total money invested out of pocket:** ₹54,00,000 (₹54 Lakh — more than double Rahul's investment).
- **Final wealth at age 60:** **₹5,29,00,000 (₹5.29 Crore)**.

### Look Closely at What Happened
- Priya invested **₹30 Lakh more** of her own cash than Rahul.
- Priya invested **three times as much** every single month.
- Yet, Rahul ended up with **more total wealth at retirement**.

Why? Because Rahul gave his initial investments an extra 10 years to double and redouble. In the 35th and 40th year, his annual interest alone was larger than his entire lifetime out-of-pocket investment.

### The Rule of 72

A quick mental model to test compounding speed is the **Rule of 72**:
```text
Years to Double Money = 72 ÷ Expected Annual Return %
```
At a 12% expected annual return from diversified Indian equities:
```text
72 ÷ 12 = 6 Years
```
Every 6 years, your money doubles:
- Age 24: ₹5 Lakh
- Age 30: ₹10 Lakh
- Age 36: ₹20 Lakh
- Age 42: ₹40 Lakh
- Age 48: ₹80 Lakh
- Age 54: ₹1.6 Crore
- Age 60: ₹3.2 Crore

Notice that the jump from ₹1.6 Crore to ₹3.2 Crore happened in the exact same 6-year interval as the jump from ₹5 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh. That is the compounding hockey stick.

### Your Action Plan for This Weekend
Do not wait for a giant salary hike. Start with whatever you can spare without starving—even ₹500 or ₹1,000 per month. You are not buying riches today; you are buying the priceless gift of 10 extra compounding years.
