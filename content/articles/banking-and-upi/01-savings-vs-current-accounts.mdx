When you open your first bank account after turning 18 or receiving a college stipend, the banker will hand you a stack of paperwork. The fundamental choice boils down to two options: a Savings Account or a Current Account.

For 99% of young adults, freelancers, and salaried employees, the answer is simple: you need a **Savings Account**.

### The Core Difference

A savings account is designed for individuals. When you deposit money, the bank gives you a modest interest return—typically between 2.5% and 3.5% per annum in major banks. 

In contrast, a current account is designed exclusively for business entities, traders, and registered firms. Because businesses make dozens or hundreds of payments every day, current accounts offer unlimited daily transactions, overdraft facilities, but **zero interest**. 

If you leave ₹1,00,000 in a current account for five years, it generates exactly ₹0 in return.

### What Is the Auto-Sweep Facility?

One of the biggest leaks for young professionals is leaving excess funds sitting in a standard savings account. 

Most banks offer an **Auto-Sweep** (or Multi-Option Deposit) feature. Here is how it works:
1. You set a threshold limit (for instance, ₹25,000).
2. Any money in your account above ₹25,000 automatically sweeps into a Fixed Deposit (FD) earning roughly 6.5% to 7.0%.
3. If you ever swipe your debit card or pay rent exceeding your liquid balance, the bank automatically liquidates only the exact required amount with zero penalty.

You get the safety and liquidity of a savings account paired with the yield of a fixed deposit.

### How Safe is Your Bank?

Every account holder in an Indian scheduled commercial bank is legally protected by the <Term id="dicgc">DICGC</Term> (a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India).

Under DICGC rules, deposits across principal and interest are insured up to **₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank**. If you accumulate substantial liquid cash exceeding ₹5 Lakh, standard practice is to spread deposits across two distinct commercial banks.

### Checklist Before Opening Your First Account
- Choose a bank with reliable mobile banking and clean UPI integration.
- Ensure the account has zero or low Minimum Average Balance (MAB) requirements.
- Verify that your mobile number and PAN card are seeded accurately to avoid KYC blocks.
